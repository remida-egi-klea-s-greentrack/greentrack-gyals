# Heating and cooling: thermostat settings

Applies to: air conditioning on the water heater and air conditioning sub-meter (sub_metering_3), and any electric heating in the "other" part of the consumption.

Heating and cooling are the largest energy use in a typical home: ENERGY STAR estimates almost half of a household's energy bill.

Advice:
- Turn the thermostat back by about 4-6 °C (7-10 °F) for 8 hours a day, for example at night or while the home is empty. The U.S. Department of Energy estimates savings of up to 10 % a year on heating and cooling.
- In summer, set the air conditioning a few degrees higher when you are home and higher still when you are away; close doors and windows while it runs.
- A programmable or smart thermostat makes these setbacks automatic. Homes with heat pumps or slow systems such as radiant floors need a thermostat and schedule suited to them.
- Use curtains: open them on sunny winter days and close them on hot summer days.

Sources: U.S. Department of Energy, Energy Saver, "Thermostats"; ENERGY STAR, "How to Save Energy at Home" (2024).
