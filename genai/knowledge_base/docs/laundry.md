# Laundry: washing temperature, full loads and drying

Applies to: the laundry sub-meter (sub_metering_2: washing machine, tumble dryer, refrigerator and a light).

Most of the energy a washing machine uses goes into heating the water, not into turning the drum: ENERGY STAR puts water heating at about 90 % of a clothes washer's energy.

Advice:
- Wash in cold water with a cold-water detergent whenever the load allows it. Moving from a hot to a warm setting alone can roughly halve the energy of a load (U.S. Department of Energy).
- Wash and dry full loads. For a small load, choose the matching water-level or load-size setting.
- Dry loads back to back, so the dryer starts warm, and clean the lint filter after every load.
- Air-dry clothes when possible. When replacing a dryer, heat pump dryers can use 20-60 % less energy than conventional ones (U.S. Department of Energy).
- Remember the refrigerator is on the same sub-meter: a steady baseline on sub_metering_2 at night is usually the fridge, not laundry.

Sources: U.S. Department of Energy, Energy Saver, "Laundry"; ENERGY STAR, clothes washers.
