# Standby power and night-time baseload

Applies to: consumption not covered by any sub-meter ("other": lighting, electronics, chargers, heating outside the kitchen and laundry) and to unusual night hours flagged by the anomaly detectors.

Many devices draw power when switched off or in standby: televisions and set-top boxes, game consoles, computers, printers, chargers and anything with a clock or remote. The U.S. Department of Energy estimates standby power at 5-10 % of residential energy use.

Advice:
- Read your own baseload: the lowest hours of the night show what is always on. Compare a quiet night with the household's typical night (hourly profile) before looking for a cause.
- If a night hour is flagged as unusual, check what could have run: a forgotten oven or dryer, a water heater reheating, electric heating, or a device left on.
- Plug entertainment and office equipment into a power strip with a switch and turn it off when not in use.
- Unplug chargers that are not charging and switch off devices fully instead of leaving them in standby.
- When buying electronics, prefer ENERGY STAR or similarly labelled models, which have lower standby power.

Source: U.S. Department of Energy, Energy Saver, standby power.
