# Water heating: thermostat and hot water use

Applies to: the water heater and air conditioning sub-meter (sub_metering_3).

An electric storage water heater keeps a full tank hot around the clock, so it uses energy even when nobody draws hot water (standby heat loss). Two levers reduce this: a lower tank temperature and less hot water use.

Advice:
- Set the water heater thermostat to about 49 °C (120 °F). Many heaters leave the factory at about 60 °C (140 °F); the higher setting loses more heat while standing and raises the risk of scalding (ENERGY STAR). Check the manufacturer's guidance before changing it.
- Each 5-6 °C (10 °F) reduction of the thermostat setting saves roughly 3-5 % of water heating costs (U.S. Department of Energy, Energy Saver).
- Use less hot water: shorter showers, full loads in the washing machine and dishwasher, and cold-water laundry where possible.
- Insulate an older storage tank and the first metres of hot water pipe, without covering the thermostat or controls.
- If the heater has a timer or off-peak tariff, heat the tank in fewer, planned periods instead of keeping it hot all day.

Sources: ENERGY STAR, "How to Save Energy at Home" (2024); U.S. Department of Energy, Energy Saver, water heating tips.
